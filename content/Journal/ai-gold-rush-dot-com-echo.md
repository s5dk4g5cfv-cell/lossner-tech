# **The AI Gold Rush and the Dot-Com Echo**

Everywhere I look, corporations are announcing bold AI initiatives. Leaders on stage promise that artificial intelligence will transform their business, reinvent their industry, and unlock staggering efficiency. The headlines are bright. The press releases are shiny.

And yet, underneath all the noise, the numbers tell a different story: **roughly 95% of corporate AI projects this year have failed**. Not stumbled, not partially delivered—failed outright. No measurable return. No working adoption. Just money sunk into pilots that quietly disappeared into the basement of “innovation labs.”

Why? Because most companies are in a rush. They’re chasing the hype. They’re lunging at the pot of gold at the end of the AI rainbow—without a map, without a shovel, and without even deciding what they would do if they actually found the treasure.

If this all sounds familiar, that’s because it is. We’ve been here before.

---
## **The Pattern of Rushing Blind**

Let’s break down why these AI projects are falling apart. It isn’t because AI is useless—it’s because corporations don’t understand what it actually takes to integrate a new paradigm.

- **Messy data.** If your foundation is sand, you can’t build a tower. Most companies have data spread across silos, systems, and spreadsheets, riddled with errors. Plugging AI into that mess only magnifies the confusion.
    
- **No clear goals.** Many pilots begin with, “We need an AI strategy!” but never specify what problem they’re trying to solve. That’s like setting out to sea without deciding which shore you’re sailing toward.
    
- **Ownership void.** These projects often have no clear owner. IT teams experiment, business leaders cheer from the sidelines, but nobody with authority takes responsibility for redesigning the workflow. The pilot floats in limbo until it dies.
    
- **Governance afterthought.** Privacy, risk, and compliance questions get asked _after_ the demo, which means the demo never goes anywhere.
    
- **Build-first reflex.** Instead of buying proven tools and adapting them, companies try to reinvent the wheel. The wheel breaks.
    

The result is predictable: bright demos in conference rooms, applause from executives, and then silence. The pilot quietly vanishes.

---
## **Echoes from the Dot-Com Bubble**

This isn’t the first time we’ve seen a frenzy like this. At the turn of the millennium, the internet was the shiny new thing. Every company rushed to “get online.” It didn’t matter if they had a plan. You just needed a website and a “.com” in your name.

The promise was intoxicating: unlimited reach, instant commerce, the death of distance. Investors threw billions into companies with no working business model.

And then the bubble burst.

The vast majority of those early internet companies collapsed. Websites with nothing but an idea and a vague promise burned out. Stock prices imploded. Dreams evaporated.

But look carefully at the survivors. Amazon, Google, eBay—they weren’t the flashiest players. They weren’t first out of the gate. They weren’t chasing hype. They were solving clear, concrete problems:

- Amazon made it easy to buy books online, then scaled methodically.
    
- Google organized the chaos of the web with search.
    
- eBay created a marketplace where strangers could safely trade.
    

In other words: they focused on fundamentals. They played the long game.

Today’s AI hype is rhyming with 2000. The same rush. The same panic. The same blindness. And eventually, the same reckoning.

---
## **The Illusion of Speed**

We live in a culture that worships speed. Move fast and break things. Blitzscale. Fail fast. The slogans are everywhere. But there’s a subtle trick buried in them: they confuse motion with progress.

In truth, speed without direction just means you crash sooner.

That’s what these AI projects are doing—crashing faster. They sprint into pilots with no clarity, no coherence, no plan. They believe that being “first” is all that matters. But in technology, as in history, being first isn’t the same as being right.

The dot-com crash proved that. The AI bust will prove it again.

---
## **Slow Is Smooth, Smooth Is Fast**

Here’s the alternative: slow down. Move with clarity. Define the problem first. Decide how you’ll measure success. Clean the data. Appoint someone who actually owns the workflow.

This doesn’t sound glamorous. It doesn’t sell newspapers. It doesn’t get you standing ovations at industry conferences. But it works.

There’s a saying from special operations teams: _“Slow is smooth, smooth is fast.”_ The idea is simple: when you take the time to do it right, you avoid costly mistakes that force you to redo everything later. In the end, you move faster precisely because you weren’t in such a rush.

AI projects should follow the same rule. Start with one clear problem. Document it in a single page. Define the metrics that will prove whether the AI is helping. Clean a narrow slice of data that actually fits the problem. Test. Learn. Scale only when you’ve delivered something real.

This is not the path of glory. It’s the path of survival. And eventually, the path of success.

---
## **Lessons Woven Together**

The hammer teaches us that AI is not the threat; our values are. A hammer can build shelter or smash a skull—it depends on who’s holding it. AI is the same.

The dot-com bubble teaches us that rushing headlong into hype ends in collapse, but those who focus on fundamentals shape the future.

Wayne Gretzky teaches us to skate not to where the puck is, but to where it’s going. Chasing today’s AI headlines is skating to where the puck has already been. Preparing your foundations—health, literacy, coherence—is skating to where it will be.

Together, these lessons form a single truth: the future doesn’t belong to the fastest—it belongs to the most coherent.

## **My Own Positioning**

- **Health** as the foundation. Without it, nothing else sustains.
    
- **AI & Systems Literacy** as the bridge. Enough to steer AI, not to compete with it.
    
- **Coherenceist Philosophy & Creative Work** as the legacy. My own contribution, my voice.
    
- **Strategic Readiness** so I’m not caught flat-footed. A playbook, a planning ritual, a way to translate.
    
- **Minimalist Digital Environment** as the tool layer. Keep it simple, coherent, portable.
    

That’s my way of moving slow, smooth, and coherent in a world obsessed with moving fast.

---
## **Closing Reflection**

The headlines will keep coming. Corporations will keep announcing bold AI pilots. Investors will keep betting on gold mines that don’t exist. And the failure rate will stay high—because hype is louder than patience, and speed is easier than coherence.

But the story doesn’t end with collapse. Just like the dot-com bubble, this frenzy will pass, and from the rubble a few durable paths will emerge. Those who play the long game—those who focus on clarity, coherence, and values—will be the ones who shape the next era.

I don’t need to chase fool’s gold. I’d rather be the builder laying foundations. The future belongs not to those who sprint blindly into the mine, but to those who know why they’re digging at all.
